# Top US-focused personal-finance / money YouTube channels and videos, 2023–2026

Data notes: Unless stated otherwise, channel stats come from vidIQ public channel pages fetched on 25–26 Nov 2026 (MagnatesMedia: 18 Nov 2026). vidIQ rounds subscriber counts, and its monthly subscriber gains are shown in 10K steps for large channels, so "0" can mean under 5K. View counts are lifetime totals for each video on the fetch date. vidIQ "most popular" lists are all-time, so pre-2023 videos are flagged as **(pre-2023)**. vidIQ does not give video lengths except on the Caleb Hammer videos page.

## 1. Which finance channels grew fastest 2023–2026? (verified numbers)

### Takeaway
In 2025–2026 the clear growth leaders are **Mark Tilbury** (9.47M subs, +160K–250K subs/month and 48M–170M views/month in mid/late 2026, driven by "I tried AI/make money as a teen" videos) and **Caleb Hammer / Financial Audit** (about 2.07M subs in May 2025 → 4.15M in Nov 2026, so roughly double in 18 months, with drama-led long-form audits plus Shorts). Mid-size explainer and faceless-style channels (How Money Works, Economics Explained, Moon, Logically Answered, Plain Bagel, Two Cents) grow slowly now, adding about 1K–30K subs/month. Several 2021–22 breakout faceless or documentary channels have stalled: MagnatesMedia, Jake Tran, Alux and Practical Wisdom are flat or near-zero.

### Inferences
- 2025–26 growth goes to (a) **beginner "how to make money / teen / AI side hustle"** framing (Tilbury) and (b) **human drama around money** (Caleb Hammer). Both are story- or emotion-led rather than pure explainers, which fits the client's story-driven "What happens if…" format.
- Mature personal-finance talking heads (Graham Stephan, Humphrey Yang, Minority Mindset) have plateaued on subs but still pull millions of views a month. Their best recent performers are **macro-fear/news hooks** ("America's $39T debt", "Oil shock", "China breaks the dollar").
- Pure faceless listicle channels from the 2020–22 era (Alux, Practical Wisdom) are stagnant. Faceless **narrative/documentary** channels (How Money Works, Moon, Logically Answered) are still growing modestly, so story beats generic listicles.

## 3. Faceless finance channels (animation / AI / stock / motion graphics) that broke out

### Takeaway
The largest faceless money channels are **Alux.com (5.1M)**, **Economics Explained (2.9M)**, **Moon (1.81M)**, **How Money Works (1.77M)**, **MagnatesMedia (1.86M)**, **Jake Tran (1.85M, mostly VO)**, **Plain Bagel (1.23M)**, **Practical Wisdom (1.04M)**, **Logically Answered (930K)** and **Wall Street Millennial (372K)**. Most broke out in 2020–2022. In 2025–26 only the story/documentary ones still add subs. I found **no verified faceless finance channel built around a recurring animated mascot character** (Tyler-style) at million-sub scale. The nearest format analog is Zack D. Films ("what would happen if" 3D-animated Shorts, about 29M subs, not finance).

### Cited Findings
- Faceless-channel stats: see table in section 1 (all [vidIQ](https://vidiq.com/youtube-stats/channel/@howmoneyworks/) pages cited there).
- Zack D. Films: short 3D animations answering a strange fact or a "what would happen if" question; about 29M subs and 79B+ views (2026 blog figure, not verified) — [TaleTok](https://taletok.io/blog/best-faceless-youtube-channels/) via search summary
- Chris Invest: whiteboard-animation finance; estimated $5K–6K/month (unsourced estimate) — [Flarecut](https://flarecut.com/blog/faceless-finance-channels/)
- Economics Explained: animated explainers with a narrator (GDP, inflation, trade) — [Flarecut](https://flarecut.com/blog/faceless-finance-channels/)
- The Plain Bagel style: animated charts + stock footage + narration — [search summary of listicles](https://outlierkit.com/resources/faceless-youtube-channels/)
- Two Cents (PBS) combines hosts with animated explainers. Its top recent video "How 'Learn to Code' Backfired on a Whole Generation" got 607K views (Sep 2025) — [vidIQ](https://vidiq.com/youtube-stats/channel/@twocentspbs/)
- Kurzgesagt (23.3M) and Bright Side (44.7M) show the scale ceiling for animated channels, but they are not finance — [search summary](https://vidpros.com/top-faceless-youtube-channels/)

### Inferences
- A white space appears to exist: a **story-driven animated character in personal-finance hypotheticals** is under-supplied at scale. Proven demand for the components is clear: "what would happen if" animation (Zack D. Films), hypothetical finance questions (How Money Works, Alux), and character-driven money drama (Caleb Hammer).
- Faceless listicle format is decaying (Alux, Practical Wisdom flat), so a recurring character and narrative arc is a plausible differentiator.
- Typical "breakout" faceless finance video ceiling seen here: 2.5M–5M views. Most uploads on mid-size faceless channels probably land at 100K–1M (estimate from monthly view totals ÷ upload cadence; not directly measured).

### Gaps
- No data found on AI-visual (generative) finance channels at scale. Vendor blogs make claims but name no verifiable channels.
- No creator interviews (Colin and Samir, Creator Science) were retrieved for these channels.

## 4. Typical view/sub ratios and RPM/CPM for US personal finance

### Takeaway
US personal-finance long-form RPM is commonly reported at **about $10–$30 per 1,000 views** (CPM about $16–$42). Credit-card, tax and investing topics sit at the top, and generic "save money" tips and side hustles sit lower. Shorts RPM is roughly **$0.30–0.40**. All published ranges come from creator-economy blogs, not YouTube. The one creator-documented case (Joshua Mayo) shows monthly RPM swinging **$6–$29.30**.

### Cited Findings
- US finance RPM $15–$26, CPM $24–$42 — [OutlierKit](https://outlierkit.com/blog/youtube-rpm-finance-niche)
- Sub-niche RPM (OutlierKit, not explicitly US-only): credit card reviews $16–26 (top $36–46); personal finance & budgeting $11–19 (top $26–32); investing $13–21; tax $13–19; retirement $12–18; real estate $11–17; debt/credit repair $10–16; crypto $9–16; side hustles $9–15 — [OutlierKit](https://outlierkit.com/blog/youtube-rpm-finance-niche)
- Joshua Mayo (personal finance): monthly RPM $6–$29.30; first-year YouTube revenue $613,960 (creator-documented, per article; date unspecified) — [OutlierKit](https://outlierkit.com/blog/youtube-rpm-finance-niche)
- Ali Abdaal: RPM grew from $0.85 to $18 as the channel matured (unsourced in article) — [OutlierKit](https://outlierkit.com/blog/youtube-rpm-finance-niche)
