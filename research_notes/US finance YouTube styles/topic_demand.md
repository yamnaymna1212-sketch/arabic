# US Money Topic Demand, Late 2025 to 2026, for a "What happens if...?" story channel

Method note: news and data stats come from web sources, dated inline. The YouTube numbers come from my own scrape of YouTube search results on **2026-11-26** (US English, the first ~20 relevance-ranked results per query, view counts as shown on that day). "Median" means the median views of those top ~20 results. A high median means many videos on the topic get views (proven demand and also crowding). A low median with one big outlier means demand exists but few channels serve it well. Source for every YouTube row: [YouTube search](https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=) plus the query shown. I could not reach Reddit (its API refused unauthenticated requests) or Google Trends directly, so Reddit activity and raw Trends curves are gaps.

## 1. What are Americans most anxious or curious about with money right now (late 2025 to 2026)?

### Takeaway
Distress is broad but uneven. Household debt is at a record $19.1T. Delinquency (5.0% of debt) is at its highest since 2016. Bankruptcies are up about 11 to 14% YoY. Record shares are taking 401(k) hardship withdrawals, and BNPL late payments are climbing. The 2026-specific shocks are ACA premiums more than doubling after the subsidies expired, the student-loan collections stop-and-start, AI-attributed layoffs, and the proposed 10% credit-card APR cap. Distress searches (debt relief, bankruptcy, can't pay rent) hit a record composite index level in Feb 2026.

### Cited Findings
**Macro debt picture**
- NY Fed Q3 2026 (released Nov 10, 2026): total household debt $19.105T, up $334B QoQ. Credit cards $1.285T, auto $1.734T, student loans $1.656T, HELOC $481B ("the largest increase in HELOC balances observed since 2005"). 5.0% of debt is delinquent, up 0.3 pts QoQ — [NY Fed press release](https://www.newyorkfed.org/newsevents/news/research/2026/20261110)
- The unrounded delinquency figure of 4.96% is the highest since Q1 2016 — [TheStreet Pro](https://pro.thestreet.com/market-commentary/household-debt-jumps-the-most-since-2022-delinquencies-climb-to-highest-since-2016)
- Annual flow into serious (90+ day) delinquency, Q3 2025 to Q3 2026: credit cards 7.05% to 6.87%, auto 2.99% to 3.02%, student loans 14.26% to 7.72%, mortgages 1.28% to 1.60% (mortgages are the only category clearly worsening) — [NY Fed](https://www.newyorkfed.org/newsevents/news/research/2026/20261110)
- The NY Fed finds the credit-card delinquency gap between low- and high-income areas widened after 2022 (Nov 2026) — [Liberty Street Economics](https://libertystreeteconomics.newyorkfed.org/2026/11/a-look-at-the-wider-divide-in-credit-card-delinquency-by-income/)
- Caveat: the NY Fed switched to VantageScore 4.0 from 2026Q1, so score comparisons with earlier reports are not like-for-like — [NY Fed HHDC Q3 2026 PDF](https://www.newyorkfed.org/medialibrary/interactives/householdcredit/data/pdf/hhdc_2026q3.pdf)
- Fed SHED (survey fielded Oct 2025, published May 13, 2026): 63% would cover a $400 emergency with cash, unchanged from 2024 and down from 68% in 2021. 12% could not pay it by any means. 4 in 10 adults earning under $50k could not cover even $100 from savings — [Federal Reserve SHED exec summary](https://www.federalreserve.gov/publications/2026-economic-well-being-of-us-households-in-2025-executive-summary.htm)

**Student loans: collections stop-and-start**
- Dec 2025: ED said wage garnishment would resume in early 2026 for about 5.5M defaulted borrowers. AEI counted about 12M delinquent or defaulted borrowers (more than 1 in 4) — [NPR, Dec 23 2025](https://npr.org/2025/12/23/nx-s1-5653312/student-loans-default-education-department)
- Garnishment can take up to 15% of disposable pay. Notices first went to 195,000 borrowers, with all 5.3M defaulted borrowers eventually to receive Treasury notices — [Fortune, Jan 7 2026](https://fortune.com/2026/01/07/student-loan-borrowers-wage-garnishment-who-is-affected-how-much-can-government-take/)
- Jan 16, 2026: ED delayed involuntary collections (wage garnishment AND Treasury Offset, i.e., tax refund seizure) with no end date — [ED press release](https://www.ed.gov/about/news/press-release/us-department-of-education-delays-involuntary-collections-amid-ongoing-student-loan-repayment-improvements); [Axios](https://axios.com/2026/01/16/student-loans-wage-garnishment-trump-collections)
- One outlet reports garnishment restarting in fall 2026, after a roughly 90-day window to enroll in the new RAP plan that launched July 1, 2026. I found no official confirmation — [The College Investor](https://thecollegeinvestor.com/80311/wage-garnishment-on-defaulted-student-loans-restarts-this-fall/)
- About 2.6M borrowers more than 120 days past due were transferred to ED's Default Resolution Group in 2026 — [NY Fed Q3 2026 search summary / HHDC](https://www.newyorkfed.org/medialibrary/interactives/householdcredit/data/pdf/hhdc_2026q3.pdf)

**Bankruptcy**
- Epiq/ABI: Q1 2026 consumer filings were 141,573 (+14% YoY). H1 2026 individual filings were 293,265 (+12%). Total filings through September were 469,719 (+11%). October 2026 individual filings were 51,266 (+2%). Full-year 2025 consumer filings were 533,949 (+12%) — [ABI/Epiq press releases](https://www.abi.org/node/1004659); [CBS News](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/bankruptcy-filing-rise-consumer-business-epiq)
- LendingTree: personal bankruptcy filings are up about 47 to 50% since 2022 (Oct 2026) — [LendingTree study](https://www.lendingtree.com/Debt-Consolidation/Personal-Bankruptcy-Study/); [Hawaii News Now](https://www.hawaiinewsnow.com/2026/10/01/personal-bankruptcy-filings-up-nearly-50-since-2022-analysis-finds/)
- A composite Google Trends index ("debt relief", "bankruptcy", "can't pay rent", "behind on mortgage") hit 64 in Feb 2026, its highest since tracking began. This is a derived index from an advocacy site — [American Default "Search Signal"](https://americandefault.org/indicators/the-search-signal/)

**Auto loans and repossessions**
- Fitch's 60+ day subprime auto ABS delinquency index hit a record 6.90% (January 2026 reading), with annualized net losses at 9.81% — [Auto Remarketing / Fitch](https://www.autoremarketing.com/?p=107747)
- Repossession estimates conflict. One outlet says 1.73M repos in 2024 (most since 2009), while NPR Planet Money estimated about 3M in 2025 — [Jalopnik](https://www.jalopnik.com/2229862/repossessions-skyrocketing-buyers-cant-afford-cars/); [NPR Planet Money, Feb 2026](https://www.npr.org/2026/02/04/nx-s1-5698499/three-sides-of-a-car-loan-update). **BNPL**
- LendingTree 2026 surveys (March 2026, about 2,000 adults): 47% of BNPL users paid late in the past year (41% in 2025, 34% in 2024). 54% say they couldn't make ends meet without BNPL. Grocery BNPL use doubled from 14% (2024) to 29% (2026) — [LendingTree BNPL stats](https://www.lendingtree.com/personal/buy-now-pay-later-loan-statistics/); [KSL](https://www.ksl.com/article/news/business/more-shoppers-using-short-term-loans-for-essentials-heres-how-to-keep-them-in-check/51497034)
- Nov 16, 2026 local news: "More people are using BNPL for basic expenses" — [Cleveland19](https://www.cleveland19.com/2026/11/16/more-people-are-using-buy-now-pay-later-basic-expenses/)

**Credit card APRs and the 10% cap proposal**
- Jan 2026: Trump called for a one-year 10% cap on card APRs starting Jan 20. Its legal authority is unclear and banks oppose it. The Sanders-Hawley (5-year cap) and AOC-Luna House bills exist — [NPR, Jan 12 2026](https://www.npr.org/2026/01/12/nx-s1-5675151/trump-credit-card-interest-rate-cap); [Fortune](https://fortune.com/2026/01/10/credit-card-interest-rate-cap-10-percent-trump-sanders-hawley-bill-consumer-harm/)

(2 days old) — YouTube search "credit card interest rate cap 10%", scraped 2026-11-26

**401(k) hardship withdrawals**
- Vanguard How America Saves 2026: a record 6% of participants took hardship withdrawals in 2025 (about 4.8 to 5% in 2024, about 2% pre-pandemic). Top reasons: avoiding foreclosure or eviction (36%), medical (31%), tuition (13%). Median balance $44,115 — [CBS News](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/401k-hardship-withdrawals-rise-vanguard-report); [Axios, Mar 4 2026](https://www.axios.com/2026/03/04/401k-retirement-plan-withdrawal-vanguard); [CNBC Select](https://www.cnbc.com/select/401k-hardship-withdrawals/)

**Health insurance (ACA subsidy cliff), a major 2026 shock**
- Enhanced ACA tax credits expired at the start of 2026. KFF estimated average enrollee premium payments would rise 114% ($888 to $1,904/yr). Sign-ups fell more than 1M to 23.1M, and effectuated enrollment fell about 13%. 9% of 2025 enrollees became uninsured — [KFF](https://www.kff.org/affordable-care-act/issue-brief/what-we-know-so-far-about-2026-aca-marketplace-enrollment-premiums-and-deductibles/); [KFF quick insight](https://www.kff.org/quick-insights/aca-marketplace-enrollment-is-down-by-3-million-after-big-jump-in-premium-payments/)
- NPR (June 26, 2026): "5 million have dropped ACA insurance" — [NPR](https://www.npr.org/2026/06/26/nx-s1-5860746/aca-health-insurance-subsidies-rates-premiums)
- This was a midterm issue. The House passed a three-year subsidy extension, with the Senate pending (per KFF midterm update) — [KFF midterm update](https://www.kff.org/health-reform/issue-brief/midterm-election-update-the-affordable-care-act-marketplaces/)

**Medical debt**
- The CFPB rule removing medical debt from credit reports was vacated on July 11, 2025 (E.D. Tex.). The court also said FCRA preempts state bans (15 states had them), though the scope of preemption is contested — [NPR, Jul 15 2025](https://npr.org/2025/07/15/nx-s1-5468438/medical-debt-credit-reports-ruling); [Brownstein](https://bhfs.com/insight/federal-court-vacates-cfpbs-medical-debt-rule-finds-fcra-preempts-state-laws)

**Layoffs and AI**
- Challenger: employers cited AI for 121,401 announced US job cuts through Oct 2026, more than double all of 2025 (54,836). AI was the top reason in May 2026 (a record 38,579, about 40% of cuts), but by October AI was cited for only 1,265. Total 2026 cuts YTD were 617,189, down 44% YoY, and Oct 2026 had 43,994 cuts, the lowest October since 2023 — [HCAmag](https://www.hcamag.com/us/specialization/transformation/ai-blamed-for-121401-job-cuts-but-the-labor-data-isnt-buying-it/594129); [TheStreet Pro](https://pro.thestreet.com/market-commentary/october-job-cuts-at-3-year-low-as-focus-turns-to-economic-conditions)
- Context: October 2025 had 153,074 cuts, the highest October in 22 years — [Challenger](https://www.challengergray.com/?p=27438)

**Homeowners insurance**
- NAIC data show nonrenewals rose 96 to 216% by region from 2018 to 2024. LendingTree (via Newsweek) estimates about 1 in 7 owner-occupied homes are uninsured, with West Virginia highest at 23.9%. Kin's 2026 midyear survey found 37% of homeowners aren't confident they can keep adequate coverage through 2026 — [The Close (NAIC)](https://theclose.com/news-homeowners-insurance-crisis/); [Newsweek](https://www.newsweek.com/map-reveals-regions-insurers-dropping-homeowners-record-rates-12341750); [Kin](https://www.kin.com/blog/kin-2026-midyear-homeownership-report/)
- About 20% of Florida homeowners go uninsured after a nonrenewal (2025) — [WUSF](https://www.wusf.org/economy-business/2025-07-22/florida-leads-nation-home-insurance-non-renewal-rates)

### Inferences
- The strongest "anxiety plus news hook" topics for late 2026 into Q1 2027 are: credit-card debt and lawsuits (with the APR-cap news), student-loan garnishment and tax-refund seizure (collections restarting), ACA premium shock and medical debt, bankruptcy, car repossession, BNPL, 401(k) raids, and HOA and home-insurance squeezes. Mortgage delinquency is the only NY Fed category clearly worsening, which suggests a "missed mortgage payment, then foreclosure" story is an early-mover topic for 2027.
- AI-layoff fear peaked in mid-2026 (May) and is fading in the data, though YouTube interest is still high. Treat it as evergreen dread rather than a breaking story.

### Gaps
- I could not get the current official status (as of Nov 2026) of student-loan wage garnishment or Treasury offset. The fall 2026 restart comes from one outlet.
- No raw Google Trends values (direct access not attempted or blocked). Only the derived composite index above.
- No confirmed outcome for the 10% APR cap (enacted or not) as of Nov 2026.
- No 2026 data found on tariff pass-through to prices, the "no tax on tips/overtime" refund impact in the 2026 filing season, Social Security 2027 COLA, sports-betting debt prevalence, or identity-theft/scam loss totals (FTC 2025 data). These were not searched for lack of budget.

### Inferences
- The step-by-step "What happens if..." format is validated right now. Legal Lens got 1.4M views in about a month on credit cards, and The Infographics Show uses "What Happens if..." titles (Social Security 125k, bankruptcy 818k). Most other "what happens if you stop paying X" queries have no strong modern video, which is the clearest gap.

- High-median topics (BNPL, sports betting, repo, debt collectors, lottery) are crowded but proven. Low-median topics with one big outlier (credit-card "what happens", student loans, HOA, insurance, bankruptcy) are the sweet spot.

## 3. Which topics suit a "follow one character step by step to the end" narrative? Plus the ranked list and content gaps

### Takeaway
The best fits have a legally defined escalation ladder with dates and surprising rules: the credit-card default to lawsuit to judgment to garnishment path, student-loan default (270 days) to Treasury offset and wage garnishment, car loan default to repo to deficiency judgment, the missed-mortgage and HOA-lien foreclosure path, bankruptcy (Ch. 7 vs 13 timeline), and the ACA subsidy cliff to uninsured ER visit to medical debt. Combining fresh 2026 data with weak YouTube supply gives the ranking below.

### Cited Findings (rules that create dramatic "surprising" beats)
- Student loans: default comes after more than 270 days. Garnishment can take up to 15% of disposable pay but must leave 30 times the federal minimum wage per week. Treasury Offset can seize tax refunds — [Fortune](https://fortune.com/2026/01/07/student-loan-borrowers-wage-garnishment-who-is-affected-how-much-can-government-take/); [ED](https://www.ed.gov/about/news/press-release/us-department-of-education-delays-involuntary-collections-amid-ongoing-student-loan-repayment-improvements)
- Student loans: rehabilitation takes 9 monthly payments, and loans already in garnishment generally can't be consolidated. RAP (from July 1, 2026) caps payments around 10% of discretionary income — [Newsweek](https://www.newsweek.com/student-loan-update-report-warns-that-millions-could-see-wage-garnishment-12596264); [The College Investor](https://thecollegeinvestor.com/80311/wage-garnishment-on-defaulted-student-loans-restarts-this-fall/)
- BNPL: on-time payments don't help credit scores, but late payments can be reported. Lenders often waive late fees if asked (Oct 2026 survey) — [LendingTree](https://www.lendingtree.com/personal/buy-now-pay-later-loan-statistics/)
- Medical debt: it can still appear on credit reports after the July 2025 ruling, and state bans may be preempted — [Brownstein](https://bhfs.com/insight/federal-court-vacates-cfpbs-medical-debt-rule-finds-fcra-preempts-state-laws)
- 401(k): SECURE 2.0 allows a penalty-free $1,000 emergency distribution. Hardship withdrawals are mostly to avoid eviction or foreclosure (36%) — [CNBC Select](https://www.cnbc.com/select/401k-hardship-withdrawals/)
- HOA: real 2026 cases include an HOA with $5.1M of debt trying to seize a couple's first home over fees, and HOA fees rising to $7,500/month — YouTube: [Atlanta News First](https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=hoa+foreclosure), WSOC-TV (scraped 2026-11-26)

## 4. Seasonality

### Takeaway
Tax topics (IRS, refunds, gig taxes, refund offsets for student loans) peak Jan to Apr. Holiday-debt, BNPL and credit-card topics peak in Dec to Jan, ACA and health insurance in Nov to Jan (open enrollment), and student-loan topics around policy dates (July 1 RAP launch) and in fall and back-to-school. News-driven spikes (Epiq monthly bankruptcy data, NY Fed quarterly reports in Feb, May, Aug and Nov) give recurring hooks.

### Cited Findings
- The NY Fed releases household debt reports quarterly (Feb 10, May 12, Aug 11 and Nov 10 in 2026) — [NY Fed Feb](https://www.newyorkfed.org/newsevents/news/research/2026/20260210); [May](https://www.newyorkfed.org/newsevents/news/research/2026/20260512); [Aug](https://www.newyorkfed.org/newsevents/news/research/2026/20260811); [Nov](https://www.newyorkfed.org/newsevents/news/research/2026/20261110)
- Treasury Offset (tax refund seizure) for defaulted student loans was part of the collections paused in Jan 2026, so its status matters most in tax season — [ED](https://www.ed.gov/about/news/press-release/us-department-of-education-delays-involuntary-collections-amid-ongoing-student-loan-repayment-improvements)
- The RAP repayment plan launched July 1, 2026, and garnishment is reported to follow after a roughly 90-day window — [The College Investor](https://thecollegeinvestor.com/80311/wage-garnishment-on-defaulted-student-loans-restarts-this-fall/)
- ACA open-enrollment sign-up numbers are reported in Jan to Mar (2026 sign-ups were 23.1M) — [KFF](https://www.kff.org/affordable-care-act/issue-brief/what-we-know-so-far-about-2026-aca-marketplace-enrollment-premiums-and-deductibles/)
- The composite distress-search index peaked in February 2026 — [American Default](https://americandefault.org/indicators/the-search-signal/)
- The BNPL survey and late-payment data come out each spring (March 2026 waves) — [LendingTree](https://www.lendingtree.com/personal/buy-now-pay-later-loan-statistics/)

### Inferences
- Suggested calendar: Dec to Jan for BNPL holiday debt, credit cards and the ACA cliff. Jan to Apr for IRS, gig taxes and student-loan refund seizure. Feb for bankruptcy (the distress-search peak). Spring for 401(k) (Vanguard report in March). Jul to Sep for student loans and back-to-school. Hurricane season (Jun to Nov) for home insurance. Evergreen: lottery, scams, gambling, debt after death.
